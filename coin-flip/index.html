    Flip a coin for heads or tails

    Select Flip coin to toss one virtual coin. Heads and tails each have a 50% chance on every flip. Use it to decide who starts a board game, settle a friendly choice, or run a probability experiment without finding a physical coin.

    Agree which option means heads and which means tails before you flip. Results appear as text as well as on the coin, so you do not need sound or animation to use the tool.

    Best-of-three coin flip

    Choose Best of three to run a short series. The first side to appear twice wins. Heads, heads ends the series at 2–0; heads, tails, tails ends it at 1–2 with tails winning. Each series takes two or three flips, and both sides have an equal chance of winning.

    Flip multiple coins at once

    In Single or multiple coins mode, enter any quantity from 1 to 100. Flip 2 coins for a pair of outcomes, 10 for a quick experiment, or 100 for a larger sample. Every coin is selected independently; the results show each flip and the batch's heads/tails counts.

    Session totals include all flips, including best-of-three rounds. Copy the latest batch, review the last ten batches, or clear results to start over. Reloading the page clears the session.

    Try a simple probability experiment

    First flip 10 coins and count the heads. Then flip 100. Compare the proportions rather than the raw counts: 6 heads out of 10 is 60%, while 53 out of 100 is 53%. Larger samples tend to approach 50% over time, but no individual batch is guaranteed to split evenly.

    Coin flip questions

    Is this a fair coin flip?

    Each result uses your browser's cryptographic random-number generator with two equally likely outcomes. The animation displays that result; it does not influence which side wins.

    Does a run of heads make tails more likely next?

    No. Each flip is independent. Even after five heads, the next flip still has a 50% chance of heads and a 50% chance of tails.

    What is the chance of two heads in two flips?

    There are four equally likely ordered outcomes: heads–heads, heads–tails, tails–heads, and tails–tails. Two heads has a 1 in 4 chance, or 25%. One of each has a 50% chance because it can happen in two orders.
