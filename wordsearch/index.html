    What this exercises Recognising a word from its shape rather than reading it letter by letter — the thing fluent readers do without noticing. The English learner level uses short everyday words and tells you what each one means when you find it; the native level uses longer and less familiar ones, against a clock.

    FREE WORD SEARCH — PLAY ONLINE, NO DOWNLOAD

    A word search puzzle you play in your browser. Pick a theme, then find every word hidden in the grid. Nothing to download, no signup, and a new puzzle is generated every time — so a theme never runs out.

    HOW TO PLAY

    • Tap the first letter of a word, then tap the last letter. No dragging — which matters on a phone, where dragging a diagonal across small letters is fiddly and inaccurate.
    • Words run across, down, or diagonally — never backwards. That's true at both levels, so you never have to read a grid in reverse.
    • Tapping the last letter first is fine too. The line is what matters, not the direction you read it in.
    • Find every word to finish the puzzle. The list beside the grid crosses them off as you go.

    TWO LEVELS

    English learner uses a 10×10 grid and eight short everyday words, with no timer. When you find a word, it tells you what the word means — so the vocabulary is the reward for finding it rather than a test you have to sit.

    Native speaker uses a 13×13 grid and fourteen longer, less familiar words, against a clock. Same themes, much less generous.

    QUESTIONS

    Is it free?
    Yes, and there's no signup. Your best score stays in your own browser.

    Does it work on a phone?
    Yes. Tapping two letters is far more accurate on a small screen than dragging across them, which is why the game works that way.

    Are the puzzles always the same?
    No — each one is generated fresh, so the same theme gives you a different grid every time.

    Do words ever run backwards?
    Never, at either level.

    Is this good for learning English?
    For vocabulary recognition, yes — the learner level shows a plain meaning for every word you find. It won't teach grammar or pronunciation, and it isn't trying to.
