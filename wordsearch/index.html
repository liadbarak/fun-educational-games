Two levels

English learner — a 10×10 grid and eight short everyday words, with no timer. When you find a word it tells you what that word means, so the vocabulary is the reward for finding it rather than a test you have to sit.

Native speaker — a 13×13 grid and fourteen longer, less familiar words, against a clock. Same themes, much less generous.

How it works

  • Tap the first letter of a word, then tap the last letter. No dragging — which matters on a phone, where dragging a diagonal across small letters is fiddly and inaccurate.
  • Words run across, down or diagonally — never backwards, at either level. You never have to read a grid in reverse.
  • Tapping the last letter first is fine. The line is what matters, not the direction you read it in.
  • Printing gives you the grid exactly as it stands on screen, the word list underneath, and a full answer key on a second sheet. Generate a new grid first if you want a different one.

Questions

Is it free?
Yes, to play and to print, with no signup. Your best score stays in your own browser and is never sent anywhere.

Can I use these in a classroom?
Yes. Print as many as you like, and the answer key comes on its own sheet so you can keep it back.

Are the puzzles always the same?
No — every grid is generated fresh, so the same theme gives a different puzzle each time you load it.

Is this good for learning English?
For vocabulary recognition, yes — the learner level shows a plain meaning for every word you find. It won't teach grammar or pronunciation, and it isn't trying to.
