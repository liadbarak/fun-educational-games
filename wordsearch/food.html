FOOD WORD SEARCH

A food word search, playable here and printable for a class. Choose a level, find everything on the list, and start a fresh grid whenever you like.

Food vocabulary earns its place early in learning a language because you use it the same week you learn it — in a shop, a canteen, a menu. The English learner level sticks to that everyday set: bread, milk, rice, eggs, the words that come up in an ordinary afternoon. Each one shows a plain definition when you find it, so a word you half-recognise gets pinned down rather than guessed at.

The native level goes somewhere else entirely — ASPARAGUS, MERINGUE, AUBERGINE, GNOCCHI. Words most English speakers can eat but hesitate to spell, which is exactly what makes them hard to spot in a grid.

HOW TO PLAY

  • Tap the first letter, then the last letter. No dragging, which matters on a phone where dragging a diagonal across small letters is inaccurate.
  • Across, down or diagonally — never backwards, at either level.
  • Either order works. Tapping the last letter first is fine; it's the line that counts.

PRINT IT

Press Print this puzzle — or just Ctrl‑P — and you'll get the grid exactly as it is on screen, with the word list underneath and a full answer key on a second sheet. Generate a new grid first if you'd like a different one; whatever is on screen is what prints. No account, no watermark, nothing to download.

THE WORDS

  • EGG — a food that comes from a hen
  • RICE — small white grains you boil
  • MILK — a white drink from a cow
  • SOUP — a hot liquid food you eat with a spoon
  • CAKE — a sweet food for birthdays
  • MEAT — food that comes from an animal
  • SALT — white grains that make food taste better
  • BREAD — a food made from flour, baked in an oven
  • APPLE — a round red or green fruit
  • JUICE — a drink made from fruit
  • HONEY — a sweet food made by bees
  • CHEESE — a solid food made from milk
