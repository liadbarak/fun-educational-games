FRUIT AND VEGETABLES WORD SEARCH

A fruit and vegetables word search, playable here and printable with an answer key. Two levels, and a new grid every time you load it.

Produce is the vocabulary people use soonest after learning it — in a shop, the same afternoon. The easy level sticks to what is on every stall: apple, pear, plum, carrot, onion, potato. Each one shows a plain definition when you find it, which turns the puzzle into something closer to a vocabulary sheet than a game.

The harder level is the produce aisle nobody is confident about — COURGETTE, CELERIAC, KOHLRABI, RADICCHIO, MANGOSTEEN. Things most people have seen, some have eaten, and very few have ever had to write down.

HOW TO PLAY

  • Tap the first letter, then the last letter. No dragging, which matters on a phone where dragging a diagonal across small letters is inaccurate.
  • Across, down or diagonally — never backwards, at either level.
  • Either order works. Tapping the last letter first is fine; it's the line that counts.

PRINT IT

Press Print this puzzle — or just Ctrl‑P — and you'll get the grid exactly as it is on screen, with the word list underneath and a full answer key on a second sheet. Generate a new grid first if you'd like a different one; whatever is on screen is what prints. No account, no watermark, nothing to download.

THE WORDS

English learner level — eight of these twelve appear in any one puzzle:

  • PEAR — a fruit that is wide at the bottom
  • PLUM — a small purple fruit with a stone inside
  • PEAS — small green balls that grow in a pod
  • APPLE — a round red or green fruit
  • ONION — a round vegetable that makes you cry
  • BEANS — seeds in a long pod that you cook
  • GRAPE — a small round fruit growing in bunches
  • LEMON — a sour yellow citrus fruit
  • CARROT — a long orange root you can eat raw
  • POTATO — a brown root you roast or mash
  • TOMATO — a soft red fruit used in salads
  • BANANA — a long yellow fruit that you peel

Native speaker level — fourteen of these are used at a time:
