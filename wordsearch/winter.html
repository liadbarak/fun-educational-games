WINTER WORD SEARCH

A winter word search you can play in the browser or print as a worksheet. Pick a level, hunt down everything in the grid, and print it whenever you want it on paper — the answer key comes on its own sheet.

Winter earns its place as a theme by lasting. Unlike a holiday, it is useful from November to March, and the easy level is built out of words a beginner meets in their first winter anywhere cold: snow, ice, coat, hat, boots. Each one comes with a plain-English meaning when you find it.

The harder level is where winter gets interesting as vocabulary. TOBOGGAN, AVALANCHE, FROSTBITE, SOLSTICE — words most people know but few have had to spell recently, which is exactly the right kind of difficult for a timed grid.

HOW TO PLAY

  • Tap the first letter, then the last letter. No dragging, which matters on a phone where dragging a diagonal across small letters is inaccurate.
  • Across, down or diagonally — never backwards, at either level.
  • Either order works. Tapping the last letter first is fine; it's the line that counts.

PRINT IT

Press Print this puzzle — or just Ctrl‑P — and you'll get the grid exactly as it is on screen, with the word list underneath and a full answer key on a second sheet. Generate a new grid first if you'd like a different one; whatever is on screen is what prints. No account, no watermark, nothing to download.

THE WORDS

English learner level — eight of these twelve appear in any one puzzle:

  • SNOW — soft white flakes that fall when it is cold
  • ICE — water that has frozen hard
  • COLD — the opposite of hot
  • COAT — a warm thing you wear outdoors
  • HAT — you wear this on your head
  • FIRE — it burns and gives off heat
  • SLED — you sit on this to slide down snow
  • WARM — a little bit hot, in a nice way
  • SCARF — you wrap this around your neck
  • FROST — thin white ice on grass and windows
  • BOOTS — strong shoes that cover your ankles
  • GLOVES — you wear these on your hands

Native speaker level — fourteen of these are used at a time:
