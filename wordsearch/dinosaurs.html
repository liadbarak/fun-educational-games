DINOSAUR WORD SEARCH

A dinosaur word search, free to play and free to print. The easy level covers the parts — claws, spikes, fossils, eggs — and the hard level is the names themselves.

Those names are what makes this theme unusual. TRICERATOPS, STEGOSAURUS, PTERODACTYL, DIPLODOCUS — eleven letters of unfamiliar Greek, and yet plenty of eight-year-olds can rattle them off. Long words are not automatically hard to find in a grid, either: the longer a word is, the fewer places it can possibly fit.

The easy level goes the other way, using short concrete words about what a dinosaur actually had, which keeps it readable for beginners while staying on the same subject.

HOW TO PLAY

  • Tap the first letter, then the last letter. No dragging, which matters on a phone where dragging a diagonal across small letters is inaccurate.
  • Across, down or diagonally — never backwards, at either level.
  • Either order works. Tapping the last letter first is fine; it's the line that counts.

PRINT IT

Press Print this puzzle — or just Ctrl‑P — and you'll get the grid exactly as it is on screen, with the word list underneath and a full answer key on a second sheet. Generate a new grid first if you'd like a different one; whatever is on screen is what prints. No account, no watermark, nothing to download.

THE WORDS

English learner level — eight of these twelve appear in any one puzzle:

  • EGG — a shell with a baby animal growing inside
  • BONE — a hard white part of a skeleton
  • CLAW — a sharp curved nail on a foot
  • TAIL — the long part at the back of an animal
  • HORN — a hard point growing from the head
  • ROAR — a very loud, very deep sound
  • NEST — where eggs are kept safe
  • TOOTH — used for biting and chewing
  • SCALE — one of the small plates covering skin
  • GIANT — extremely large
  • SPIKE — a sharp pointed part sticking out
  • FOSSIL — remains that have slowly turned to stone

Native speaker level — fourteen of these are used at a time:

TRICERATOPS · STEGOSAURUS · PTERODACTYL · SPINOSAURUS · ALLOSAURUS · DIPLODOCUS · IGUANODON · CRETACEOUS · MESOZOIC · JURASSIC · TRIASSIC · PREDATOR · CARNIVORE · HERBIVORE · SKELETON · EXTINCT · AMBER · TALON
