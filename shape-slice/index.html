Shape Slice

Draw one line. Solve the shape.

How to Play Shape Slice

Read the short objective, draw one straight line, and release to slice. Your line extends across the board automatically, so a short, deliberate gesture is enough. You can also tap two points. The pieces separate to reveal what you made, with immediate feedback about their sides, area or symmetry.

Solve a challenge to unlock the next one, or choose Improve cut to chase a more accurate result. There are 32 challenges, no timer and no lives. A retry only adds points if it improves your previous score for that challenge. Strong consecutive solutions build a modest streak bonus; a failed or less accurate attempt resets the streak.

Mouse, touch and trackpad controls use the same straight-line mechanic. Keyboard players can focus the board, move the cursor with arrow keys, and press Enter to set each endpoint. Hold Shift for finer movements; Escape cancels an unfinished line.

Geometry You’ll Encounter

Practice geometry by turning familiar 2D shapes into new ones. This interactive math puzzle lets you test an idea and inspect the result immediately. A corner sliced off a rectangle can leave a pentagon. A diagonal can turn a square into two triangles. You are working with the actual boundaries of geometric shapes, rather than selecting a name from a list.

Some challenges care about sides and vertices; others ask for equal area, a particular proportion, or mirror-image pieces. Marked targets and protected regions add another question: can the same straight line meet every condition?

2D Shapes Guide

Understanding Sides and Vertices

A side is a straight boundary segment. A vertex is a corner where two sides meet. Every simple polygon has the same number of sides and vertices. Adding a point along an existing straight edge does not create a new side: the direction must change.

Where a cut meets the boundary matters. Going through a corner can produce a different side count from crossing nearby edges. For shape-creation challenges, a line very close to a vertex gently snaps to it. Nearly parallel cuts in rectangle rounds also straighten slightly. The displayed cut and scored pieces both use the adjusted line.

Area and Equal Parts

Area measures the surface inside a shape, not the length of its boundary. Two pieces can have different outlines and still cover equal amounts of space. In a triangle, a line from a vertex to the midpoint of the opposite side creates equal areas, even when the triangles look different.

For a 25% challenge, the smaller piece should contain one quarter of the original area. A “twice the area” challenge instead asks for a one-third/two-thirds split. Percentage labels on the pieces help you judge which direction to move your next cut.

Symmetry

A line of symmetry acts like a mirror: reflecting the whole shape across it leaves the outline unchanged. Equal area alone is not enough. A rectangle has horizontal and vertical mirror lines through its center; a non-square rectangle’s diagonal divides its area equally but is not a mirror line.

Shape Slice compares the reflected outline with the original boundary. Both placement and angle affect the result, and every valid mirror line is accepted.

Shape Slice Tips

  • Count the existing edges on each side of your proposed line, then include the new cut edge.
  • For area challenges, adjust the line’s position in small steps rather than changing position and angle together.
  • Look at opposite corners when asked for triangles. Look between corners when you need more sides.
  • Check the entire extended line before avoiding a red region; a short gesture still creates a full cut.

Shape Slice FAQ

Can I use Shape Slice to practice geometry?

Yes. Challenges give you hands-on practice with polygon sides and vertices, equal areas, proportions and lines of symmetry. The shape guide explains these concepts, while the cut results let you compare your prediction with the actual geometry.

What is Shape Slice?

An original geometry game where one straight cut solves each challenge. You create polygons, balance areas, find symmetry and connect targets.

How do you play Shape Slice?

Drag across the shape or tap two endpoints. Review your pieces, then retry or continue to the next challenge.

What are geometric shapes?

They are figures described by boundaries and spatial properties. This shape game focuses on flat polygons rather than curved figures or 3D solids.

What is a polygon?

A closed, flat shape made from straight sides. Triangles, quadrilaterals, pentagons and hexagons are polygons.

What is a line of symmetry?

A line across which a shape can be reflected onto itself. A shape may have several such lines, one, or none.

How is cut accuracy calculated?

Area accuracy measures the difference between the smaller piece’s proportion and the target. Symmetry measures reflected-boundary error; target accuracy measures distance from the dots. Area rounds pass at 80% accuracy, symmetry at 85%, and targets must be within the marked circles.

Can a puzzle have more than one solution?

Yes. The game checks the resulting geometry, not whether you copied a stored answer. Hints show one possible solution.

Is Shape Slice free to play?

Yes, with no account or download. Best score and best streak save in this browser when storage is available; each new visit begins a fresh set.
